This is more an encapsulation of someone else's idea than one of my own, but in *Project Hail Mary* Andy Weir comes up with a theory that other advanced aliens are probably of similar intelligence to humans.

In it, he postulates that an intelligent species like humans is only so intelligent as to have a permanent long-term advantage over the other animals in their environment.

There's probably a mathematical function based on the speed of light, the speed of sound in the local medium, and a couple of other factors that determines how much processing power your brain needs to be able to hit a bird with a rock, or a deer with a spear; and above that there isn't really population-wide evolutionary pressure to get much smarter.

That isn't to say ancient aliens might not break evolution. A society obsessed with designer gene editing might make themselves smarter as a species to the point that they really stood out; but without knowing other species it's hard to guess what sorts of demand would make the majority of a species consistently do that sort of thing for hundreds of generations to become standout intelligent.

Rather, I wouldn't be surprised if species exist somewhere around the same sorts of standard distributions of intelligence as a scale like IQ might propose, with some having greater or smaller variance, and a higher or lower mean.

## Smarter or dumber?

It would be fascinating to see whether, once you're smart enough to be able to leave your solar system, it's actually more advantageous to be a smarter or dumber species. Smarter humans tend to reproduce at a lower rate, so maybe there's a chance that stupider aliens have greater population pressures to drive them to new worlds?

Or maybe the societies that have that pressure all blow themselves up before they leave their sun, and the more intelligent (and peaceful) societies eventually see their reproduction rate fall below replacement, and that is, in fact, the great filter.

What a lame filter that would be.
