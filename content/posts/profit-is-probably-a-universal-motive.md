This may be personal bias, but there seems to me an uncanny similarity between evolutionary pressure and the way that the profit motive seems to align. Organisms compete to eke out a niche where they are most competitive in a given environment; so do businesses and entrepreneurs struggle to eke out a niche where they can carve out some corner of the market to enable themselves, their dependents, and their stakeholders to have a comfortable living.

Evolutionary pressure is efficient: vestigial tails are lost if they require too many more calories to grow and maintain than they help bring in. So does the profit motive, in free and fair markets, lead to businesses that are theoretically as efficient as they can be.

My suspicion is that, were we to meet advanced aliens, and were they hindered by the same fundamental limitations of supply as we are (i.e. they don't have free, infinite matter à la Star Trek replicators), they would mostly allocate their resources in a way that might be comprehensible to humans.

I suspect that leveraging the evolutionary drivers to provide for one's children, to enable the construction of society writ large, will be the most common economic structure amongst our advanced aliens.

That isn't to say, however, that their economies will resemble American capitalism in the early 21st century. Rather, when dealing with such a society we can likely guess that they're inspired by personal gain, that some among them primarily shape their decision-making by a desire for personal profit, and that some flavor of cold-blooded economic rationalism might exist amongst them.

## Good news and bad news

That can be both good and bad.

I don't know what modern humans would make of a Star Trek society. I also don't know if we would ever trust aliens so beyond personal incentive to help us (and not, instead, constantly watch for the conquistador fleet).

On the other hand, a profit motive might not lead to positive outcomes for humans. An alien Cortés could decide to go off on his own to conquer us for gold and glory.

Or worse yet, the short-sightedness of their own alien economy might never allocate the resources to visit us.

## The economics of interstellar travel

From a perspective of pure economic rationalism, it is hard to justify interstellar travel. Even if the society at the other end traded you 100 tons of diamonds (or any choice commodity) for a copy of the human Wikipedia, it is hard to imagine how that would be a profitable venture even for the most advanced of spacefarers (assuming they're limited to travel at the speed of light).

And worse, what if they fall through on the trade once you get there? Our erstwhile interstellar traveler would have no negotiating leverage once they've spent three generations en route to our world, only to spend three on the way home.

Worse still, what if we do in fact live in a dark forest of hostile worlds that will kill any intelligent life they discover? Or what if your trading partner decides to send their own Cortés to you?

If profit is a universal motive among advanced aliens, it may very well be the answer to Fermi's Paradox. It might just not ever be valuable to trade physical goods between stars.

## But what about information?

An advanced alien could build a network of a sort of Von Neumann relay probe: probes that go out, self-replicate, and build a disparate network to contact potential neighbors, all while hiding their solar system of origin.

Once they made contact, onboard AI (or LLMs) could decipher a common language between themselves and our advanced alien neighbors and arrange a trade: of information.

That's probably all we have to offer, and probably all they could offer us.

It could be information about our society, our history, our music, our scientific and engineering knowledge. Such a network could be an alien society's lasting legacy for the universe long after their sun burns out and their society crumbles from nuclear war, climate change, and whatever other filters we don't yet know of.

And yet, as of writing in 2025, we know of no such relay network of alien Wikipedias.

So maybe there is an efficient filter...
