---
title: "Aliens Probably Aren't That Diverse"
date: 2025-04-15
type: Essay
abstract: "Questions the science-fiction cantina: reproductive isolation and genetic drift make stable, multi-species populations implausible, so a ship crewed by many species may indicate a rigid religious hierarchy rather than a federation of equals."
keywords: [demography, speciation, religion, science fiction]
---

Some of my favorite space operas feature incredibly diverse environs of aliens: the cantina in Mos Eisley, *Babylon 5*, *Deep Space Nine*. Scenes with a bar where some alien cocktail waitress flirts with a drunk of a different species, while being bossed around by a third, and the bouncer is some sort of giant from the planet Bouncera.

I always find myself thinking: they must have a lot of immigration to this base, because there's no chance this population is reproducing at a replacement rate.

Given that humans can't reproduce with bonobos, it seems all but impossible that we'd be able to reproduce with Klingons or Twi'leks. And that's even before accounting for the natural genetic drift that would happen from one end of an interstellar empire to another, say if the ends are separated by 50+ light-years.

## Self-segregation

While I don't presume to say that there would be no interspecies mingling (of various levels), I suspect that there is some base number of any given species that would need to exist on a given planet / asteroid / space station to allow it to sustain itself. You could have multiple species on that single planet, but I suspect that basic pressures to reproduce would lead to them self-segregating and, inevitably, coming into some sort of conflict.

There might be trading posts that are particularly diverse, but I struggle to see how their populations could be anything but transient (and prone to sudden collapse) unless they're supported by a larger host population.

## Empires of faith

A few series, *Halo* and *The Culture* amongst them, have interspecies societies that are marked by a strict hierarchy between them. In both, the societies are focused on some sort of maniacal religious caste system which has made way to support multiple planets (and species) within its prophecies.

Given human interaction, I see this outcome as upsettingly plausible. Were we to meet advanced aliens, if there were multiple species on a given ship it might be more indicative of radical religious politics than of a great interspecies federation.

Which begs the question: were we to meet such a diverse group of aliens, and if they had greater technology, should we convert willingly or at the end of an energy sword?
