Ever since I was a kid watching *Star Wars*, I've been interested in aliens from distant worlds interacting with us. As a kid, I went the UFO route, but with my father's help I was continually disappointed when their radical claims repeatedly fell in light of scientific standards. As an adult I've been amazed at the marvel of the combination of Drake's equation and an infinite universe, but I've been really disappointed in the focus on microbial alien life.

This isn't to say that the discovery of microbial alien life isn't important. Discovery of alien life at any level adds weight to the principle of mediocrity, and vindicates additional astrobiological research. The problem, however, is that the discovery of single-celled organisms on Enceladus doesn't really inspire a generation to pour resources into space exploration and colonization.

Which is why I'm primarily interested in a far more exciting type of alien life, and the conditions in which it could arise: intelligent life. To be more specific, I'm interested in two levels of intelligent life: pre-interstellar civilizations and interstellar civilizations.

## Two kinds of civilization

Pre-interstellar civilizations I define as everything from when man discovered fire until the point when we (hopefully) have a permanent presence in another solar system, and thus are no longer bound to the lifespan of our sun. Drake's equation implies a lifespan to technologically advanced civilizations, a lifespan which I believe is significantly lengthened when a civilization is in multiple solar systems.

Interstellar civilizations are like those depicted in the *Star Wars* and *Star Trek* universes, which I (hope) is the eventual fate of mankind. Part of the value of speculating about the conditions in which an interstellar civilization may arise or exist is that if an alien species were to make first contact with us, we will have a better framework with which to approach them.

## A name for the field

I have searched for a field of study focusing on these issues, and haven't been able to find anything, which is why I propose to call this field Astro-Sociology: to tie it to the established field of astrobiology, while being clear that it is not a hard science. It is speculative, and it is a study of the social behaviors resulting from different environments and how those might shape civilizations.
