One of the most interesting parts of science fiction, to me, has always been the makeup of alien societies. Whether they are "humans with stuff on their foreheads" like the Klingons; questionably advanced hive animals, like Heinlein's Bugs in *Starship Troopers*; or godlike in their otherness like Comey's Ring Builders, I find myself particularly fascinated by what advanced alien life might look like.

If mankind ever meets advanced alien life, it would probably be useful to have some structure around what we think that alien life would look like. Thus far I've found shockingly little research into this area, beyond the on-camera musings of a few astrophysicists and the occasional paper by a sociologist. That is shocking.

While it is unlikely that mankind will meet advanced aliens in my lifetime, it seems pertinent that during such a meeting there would be some "expert" with study in relevant fields who would be brought forth to try to make contact with them. The idea that that expert's primary field might be the decay of suns, or the quantum fluctuations around a black hole, or how uncontacted human tribes deal with advanced humans seems... high risk.

It seems like there should be a field, which some have proposed calling Astro-Sociobiology, that studies what advanced aliens might look like if we ever meet them.

I think there are two important ideas that should be established in such a field, namely who it should pertain to and what organizing principles it should have.

## Rule 1: Advanced aliens only

The study of this field should focus itself on "advanced aliens," as opposed to any form of alien life (which might include single-celled bacteria, plants, or even creatures as advanced as dinosaurs or dolphins). Advanced aliens should be those that mankind has at least some chance of coming into contact with in the next 100 years, which implies that they can either physically travel through the stars themselves, or can communicate from one star system to another.

I think that, under this definition, mankind might have only reached this status roughly around the 1950s, when our radios became effective enough that we could, theoretically, reach another solar system with our broadcasts.

## Rule 2: No magic

Secondly, I think this field of study is only interesting if it is bound by the rules of current science in the observable universe. That is to say, since we know of no function that could allow for psychic messages, it is extraordinarily unlikely aliens have such a faculty (sorry Vulcans, or Bugs, or Ring Builders).

I think this second rule likely implies that the laws of evolution, as we currently understand them, are most likely to have constrained the biological choices of advanced aliens; but that is a supposition all its own and worthy of study (which evolutionary constraints are universal, versus only effectively true given the conditions of Earth?).

And with that, allow me to endlessly ponder what advanced aliens of the universe might look like!
