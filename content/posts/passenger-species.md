Most of the ideas I've written about thus far presume that, to be an interesting alien, a species must evolve that way on their own. This isn't the universal trope in science fiction, and it is unlikely to be universally true throughout the universe.

The 1998 movie *Independence Day* seems to imply that most of human progress since the 1950s is from recovered alien debris. The *Stargate* series is predicated on ancient aliens having left behind a network of wormholes, with another species eventually giving humans warp capabilities. *Star Trek* ships have a rarely mentioned Cetacean Ops, decks occupied by intelligent dolphins who do their own tasks. *Star Wars'* Wookiees appear to live in a pre-industrial society, implying that Chewbacca didn't learn how to fly a spaceship on his home world.

So how should we think of these species that cannot natively develop the capacity to communicate beyond their gravity well? They could range anywhere from near misses (who lack one understanding or resource preventing them from becoming "interesting") to, on the opposite side of the spectrum, a "project species" that some other species has decided to help elevate into interesting status.

## Project species

When thinking about project species we could think about, for example, dolphins and chimps. Both are species that show all signs of being quite intelligent (some might speculate within an order of magnitude of humans, given 100,000 years of tools / writing / civilization), and both are species that some people are trying to elevate to a more advanced level (by teaching language, reading, etc.).

What if those projects succeeded? What if, in the next 300 years, we were able to help dolphins become as interesting as humans, heavily aided by tools they could never have made in the ocean (but could conceivably be made by humans for them until they could make them on their own)?

Project species are particularly challenging for attempts by Astro-Sociobiology to draw boundaries on what interesting aliens might look like. Whether it's a project heavily influenced by compassion, which I'd argue dolphins are, or one influenced by vanity (like some sort of fashionable pet that is more fashionable if self-aware), it would be very hard to predict where a project species might go.

Will the dolphins, furious at past treatment, rise up and be the successors of our corner of the universe?

## Parasites and stowaways

An altogether separate passenger species might be best thought of as a parasite. There are a number of sci-fi examples where this is specifically true (Halo's Flood, The Culture's Aultridia), but I'm actually more interested in (and more expect to be real) species that are, essentially, stowaways in an intelligent alien species and reach intelligence as such.

Raccoons are a great potential example of this. They are notoriously smart and clever, have many of the attributes (like thumbs) that helped humans, and live seamlessly within human society. Would it be so crazy, for example, to see them at an Ewok level of sophistication in 10,000 years? If human civilization fell, could they build a new one in our wake, using our radios and our rockets?
