In order to become an "interesting alien" there are basic technologies you need to have access to. If your tree of life starts somewhere that lacks the physical requirements for these basic technologies, it becomes quite the act of contrition to imagine a scenario where an alien species might get to the point that their existence might be known more than a few AU away from their planet.

## Fire

The most obvious of these technologies is, of course, fire. Fire does a couple of key things for humans:

1. It helps us prepare our food in such a way that we kill off the more obvious bacteria and parasites.
2. It staves off the cold, allowing us to live in more parts of our planet.
3. It's a good initial pass at allowing us to move things really quickly, à la gunpowder and rockets.
4. It is necessary, of course, for metallurgy, without which you'd be hard pressed to make the metals needed for a radio or a rocket.

You could contrive a species that has disinfecting chemicals in their digestive tract, but in the cat and mouse of evolution it's certain something would overcome that initial advantage. You could contrive a species better adapted to cold climates, but that would almost certainly come with a caloric disadvantage (or an inability to live in hotter climates). You could contrive a society that came up with chemical rockets before jet engines, but it seems hard to imagine so big a leap as a first step.

All of these requirements point to intelligent aliens needing to originate from a world that has burnable stuff. That is yet another reason to suspect carbon-based life: if you were silicon-based, your plant equivalents would be too, and it would be hard to burn them to disinfect your food.

## Other prerequisites

I suspect there are some other basic technologies that point to what intelligent aliens would need to be like:

- **Levers and pulleys** probably require origination on a body with gravity greater than some level. This probably has a down-the-line effect of shaping the size and movement habits of intelligent aliens.
- **Radios** probably require origination on a body with a substantial magnetosphere to protect early radios from being of no use. That probably points to what the planet, and therefore the aliens, are chemically composed of.
- **Ink**: it is hard to quickly write without something that looks like ink. If you can't quickly write, you can't spread knowledge widely. Most early inks were derived from fluids from animals or plants, which points to planets with a decent amount of water. Probably no rock-monster aliens will discover the printing press if, in fact, they can get past basic physics limitations.
- **Optics**: it is incredibly hard to imagine going to the stars (or even into orbit) without first looking at them. If you need evidence, the funding differences between optical and radio telescopes should serve as your proof. Optics require glass, which requires sand, fire and, incidentally, a strong centering on vision as a primary sense. If echolocating intelligent aliens exist, it's hard to really imagine them getting outside the range of things they can scan and bounce back to them within their lifetime.
