As a premise, I assume the laws of physics are constant throughout the universe, and that likely life is dictated by selective pressure that we would recognize as a fairly consistent set of evolutionary constraints.

## Four limbs, two thumbs

If that is so, the same efficiencies that drove vertebrate life on Earth to have four limbs would likely do the same on other worlds. Four is just a good number of limbs. If you're a quadruped, which most vertebrate land life probably descends from, you literally can lose a leg and still be 98% functional (just slow).

If you're a quadruped who starts climbing things that look like trees, you probably develop something that works like a thumb so that you can hang on to things that look like branches (if you're a plant, being taller than others and armored is a good strategy; having branches that get you more sunlight is another good strategy). That thumb is going to come in handy as you start to realize the utility of throwing things at prey and predators... which likely leads to you taking over your planet.

All of which means, if we're hoping to come across interesting aliens, they probably have two legs, two arms, and thumbs. I'd bet they also concentrate their sensing on a fourth appendage, which is close to their brain for better processing time.

Meaning they also have a head with a face. Maybe even a similar layout of sensing organs.

## Human factors, alien factors

In user experience design, there's a discipline called human factors. How does the shape of a human dictate the most natural (and least unnatural) layout for a door? How should a house be laid out? How should you fit a person into a car and get their control inputs?

If interesting aliens look kind of like us, my suspicion is that their stuff does too. They probably also use doors to separate spaces; they probably also have chairs; they probably also drink out of something that looks like a cup.

The doorknob might be placed funny (heck, even humans place their doorknobs in funny places), but it probably serves a similar purpose. More than that, if we stumbled upon an alien's dwelling we could likely figure out their physiology from their stuff. We'd probably be able to infer their height from their doors and doorknobs, their diet from their utensils, and how many hours a day they sleep from how their sleeping quarters work.

## Our first evidence will probably be garbage

Given how humans operate, if we come across physical (non-radio-wave) proof of interesting aliens, and it isn't a probe or a manned ship, it will probably be garbage. A chunk of the wreck of a ship that had a mechanical failure and has been floating our way for quite some time... or something like that hitching a ride on a trans-solar comet.

Looking at that wreck, even if it's just a part of a door, will likely tell us enough about them that we could start to meaningfully flesh out what type of society they might live under.
